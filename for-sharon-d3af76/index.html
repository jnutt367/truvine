What It Is

The Family Wall

The flock’s wall — and its three doors.

The Family Wall is a page of ours where the TruVINE family hangs together — the faces and names of folks from all over God’s earth who watch the films, read the Word with us, and belong here. Jesus said He has other sheep, and He must bring them too, until there’s one flock with one Shepherd. This wall is our little way of making room for that flock.

Counting the flock is good, and we count it carefully. But a family isn’t a number — a family has faces, and names, and room at the table. So we built a wall with room, and it stands empty until real people say yes. No made-up faces, no strangers borrowed for decoration — an honest wall, waiting.

The Three Doors — None of Them Lesser

  • Door One — your photo on the wall. Your face hangs with the family, under the name you choose — your own picture as it is, or farm-style artwork made from it, in the form you choose.
  • Door Two — your name on the roll. Counted and named, with no face shown. Some folks are private; private is honored here.
  • Door Three — the quiet door. No name, no face, nothing public at all. You’re counted by the Shepherd, loved exactly as much, and held in prayer. Anonymous is not absent — anonymous is family, kept safe.

None of the doors locks. Whatever you pick can change at any time, in either direction — one word from you, and it’s done, no questions asked. And joining is never a form that disappears into a computer: it’s a letter, and Jason reads every single letter himself.

Your Answer

Answer Right Here

You don’t have to go anywhere else — your letter to Jason starts on this very page.

Answering works the way warm things work in this family: you write Jason a letter. Fill this in, and it opens in your own mail app, addressed straight to him — nothing you write here is stored on this website. It travels like a letter, from your hands to his, and Jason reads every letter himself.

Choosing the photo door? When your letter opens in your mail app, just attach the picture you like before you send it — or tell Jason in the letter which photo, and he’ll take it from there.

After You Write

  • You pick a door — for yourself, and for Ian in whatever way you think is best for him. You decide that, always.
  • If you pick the photo door, you choose the picture — one of you that you like, that you’re happy to have hang.
  • Farm-style artwork may be made from it — and nothing hangs until you approve it. You see the artwork with your own eyes first, and only your yes puts it on the wall.
  • Then Jason hangs the household together — you, Jason, and Ian — as the first family on the wall, before the film ever invites the world.

And if you’d rather not — that’s a complete answer too, and it changes nothing. There’s no wrong answer here.
