The Standards

The whole rulebook, in plain speech. If a piece of work can’t pass these, it doesn’t ship — no matter how beautiful it is.

  • Scripture is the test. Every scene, event, and detail is verified against the Word before it is built — and the narration is Grandpa Teddy reading that Word word for word. Teddy is the only voice; no character’s lines are invented.
  • The cast comes from the sheets, always. Every figure is taken from the locked character sheets. A face once crowned is never re-invented, and no footage from outside the canon supplies a face or a body.
  • Jason crowns before anything moves. Every scene begins as stills. The true ones are crowned; the rest are rebuilt. Animation is crown-gated, and stills are never rationed when the story needs more of them.
  • Desk animation first. Crowned stills are layered and moved by hand — light, water, atmosphere. AI video generation is the exception: small, named, and counted before it is spent.
  • Sound is measured, not hoped for. Every film is normalized to −16 LUFS and verified for narration sync before it is shown as final.
  • Nothing publishes without Jason’s review. Not to YouTube, not anywhere. A build is a candidate until the shepherd has watched it and given the word.
  • The People Principle. A person’s story belongs to them. Stories, names, and faces are shared only as their owners desire — asked plainly, re-asked when the use grows, and revocable. Crew are named only as they wish to be named.

Open Needs — Ways to Serve

Real seats, honestly described. Most are small and quiet; all of them matter.

Stills graders

Patient eyes that compare each new still against the character sheets — likeness, garments, and whether the picture says what the verse says.

Caption checkers

Careful readers who follow the narration against the captions and the chapter text, catching a wrong word before a viewer ever sees it.

Prayer cover

Intercessors who hold production nights before the Lord — the first work of this studio is prayer, and it is never the leftover work.

Thumbnail eyes

Honest first-glance critics: does this frame tell the truth about the video, at a glance, on a small screen? If it looks like garbage, we don’t ship it.

If one of these seats has your name on it in your heart, raise your hand at the Crew Door on the public wall and say which one — letters arrive in Jason’s own inbox.
